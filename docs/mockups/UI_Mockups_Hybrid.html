Zen, with Coach and Cockpit that only arrive when you stop

One base: Zen is what you see while typing — the line and nothing else. Coach is the session structure, in furniture that is not on screen until you are between lines. Cockpit is the feedback, and it waits out a grace period first, so carrying straight on into the next line is never interrupted. The three flavours differ in where the furniture comes from, which turns out to change how the whole thing feels.

These are live. Click a line and type it — the real Art of War line 347. Enter finishes the line, Backspace deletes, Esc resets. Then stop typing and watch. The grace period is on a slider under each one: if you start the next line before it elapses, nothing appears at all. The state in the top-right of each frame shows TYPING → ARMED → SHOWN so you can see the mechanism working.

AWings — furniture at the edges

Coach slides in from the left, Cockpit from the right, and the line never moves: the panels overlay rather than push, because shifting the text you have just been reading is the one thing that would break the calm. Two faint nubs at the edges are the only hint they exist while you type, and hovering an edge pulls its panel in early, so you are never waiting on a timer you did not want.

BConsole — bands above and below

Nothing ever appears beside the line. The coach drops a thin band from the top — where you are in the session, one line of it — and the cockpit rises as a deck from the bottom with the numbers. Motion is vertical, so the line's horizontal measure is untouched and your eye returns to exactly the same place. It is the arrangement that disturbs the reading position least.

CBloom — the line annotates itself

No panels at all. When you stop, the line you just typed stays exactly where it is and becomes the feedback: each character re-colours by how long you took over it, so the slow patches are visible in the words themselves, and the verdict and the coach's next move appear directly beneath. Nothing arrives from off-screen, nothing to dismiss — the optical centre never moves, which is the most faithful reading of Zen.
