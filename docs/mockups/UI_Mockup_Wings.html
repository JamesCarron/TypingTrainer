Wings, refined — rails at rest, panels when you ask

The chosen direction with the sidebars cut back. While you type there is nothing but a faint nub at each edge. When you stop, and the grace period elapses, each wing appears as a slim rail carrying only what is worth a glance: which step of the session you are on, and how the last line went. Select a rail and it expands into the full panel — the session plan on the left, the instruments on the right.

Live. Click the line and type — Enter finishes, Esc resets. Stop, and the rails arrive. Then click a rail, or press ← / →, to expand it; Esc or clicking the centre collapses it. Typing anything at all puts everything away instantly.

The rule that makes it workThe centre column permanently reserves one expanded panel's width on each side, so the line sits in precisely the same place in all three states. Expanding overlays empty margin, never text, and nothing reflows under your eyes mid-session.
Why only one at a timeOpening the second wing is what would force the line narrower. Selecting one collapses the other, which also keeps the interaction honest: you are looking at the plan, or at the numbers, not at both.
Two ways to pay for the panelReserve space holds the panel's width permanently: the line never moves and is never covered, but it is capped near 55 characters and there is dead margin at rest. Overlay as a drawer gives the line its full 70-character measure and slides the panel over the top, dimming the text behind. Switch between them above — this is the one decision left in this design.
What the rails carryLeft: which step, and how far through. Right: the last line's speed and accuracy, and your three worst keys. Everything else waits until you ask for it, which is what keeps the resting state readable at a glance rather than something to study.
