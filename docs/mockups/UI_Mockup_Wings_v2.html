Wings v2 — permanent rails, drawer panels, and three homes for the deep analysis

Everything settled from the last round is in: the panel overlays as a drawer so the line keeps its full measure, the measure itself is a setting, the rails are permanent rather than appearing after a pause, and every figure on the right rail is labelled. The grace period still exists but it now governs one thing only — whether the numbers arrive, which is the part that would interrupt mid-line. Below that, the same base three ways, answering the open question: where does the deeper analysis live.

Live. Click a line and type — Enter finishes, Esc resets or closes. Click either rail to open its drawer; the left one holds the session plan and the settings, including the line width. Each of the three options below has its own working affordance for the deep analysis — labelled on the rail, so you can see how you would reach it.

0The base

The rails are always there. While you type, the right rail shows its labels with the figures held back; when you stop and the grace period elapses, the numbers fade up in place — the furniture does not move, so there is nothing to catch your eye mid-line. WPM, ACC and WORST are spelled out, because bare numerals on a 46-pixel rail read as ambiguous in v1.

What permanence costsThe rails are now always in view, which is two vertical surfaces Zen did not have. They are narrow and static, so they read as a frame rather than as content — but this is the honest trade for never having furniture appear mid-session.

ADeep analysis as a place

The rail's bottom item is a door: FULL STATS › takes over the whole surface with the analysis page — tiles, confusions, error categories, the keyboard heatmap, the learning curve. You have left the typing screen and you know it; going back is one click. This is what the app does today, wired to the rail instead of a separate route.

BDeep analysis as a layer

The wing expands twice. One click gives the drawer, as before; the drawer's own go deeper pushes it out to a full-height sheet covering most of the stage, with the typing line still visible at the edge. Esc steps back down one level at a time — sheet, drawer, closed. You never navigate away, so the analysis stays part of the session rather than an errand.

Why it might winOne continuous surface, three depths, one key to climb back down. The line stays visible at the left edge, so you never lose your place, and acting on a finding — drill this, jump there — puts you straight back to typing.
What it costsA sheet that is not quite full width and not quite a panel can read as neither. And the deep view only gets about 80% of the stage, so the widest things — a learning curve over 444 lines, a full keyboard heatmap — are cramped compared with a real page.

CDeep analysis as a moment

No door on the rail at all. The analysis is the closing act of a session: you finish, and it comes to you — what moved today, what did not, and one thing to do about it next time. The rails carry only the live figures; anything historical waits for the review. The argument is that weak-point analysis is not something you consult mid-line, it is something you reflect on when you stop.
